Help

The five things people contact the Bureau about, answered in the order they ask them — with a telephone number you can actually ring.

I want to bid for a contract

  1. Register once. You need your CAC registration number and your TIN. Registration is free and stays valid while your certificates do — start here.
  2. Keep your certificates current. Certificates carry expiry dates. A lapsed tax clearance suspends bidding automatically, so a bidder cannot discover it at the last minute in a submission room.
  3. Find the tender. Filter everything open for bids, or subscribe to the RSS feed, or watch the newspaper advertisement — all three carry the same notices.
  4. Read the pack before you price. The estimate is published, the criteria and their weights are published, and every question any bidder asks is answered in public.
  5. Submit before the deadline. Deadlines are shown in West Africa Time (UTC+1) and the platform refuses late submissions; “my network was slow” is not a ground for appeal, which is why the platform is built for a phone on 2G.

I want to check who won a contract

  • The award register lists every award with the winning company, the amount and the reason for the decision.
  • Contracts show variations and payment certifications after signature.
  • The supplier register shows whether a company is verified and whether it has been debarred.
  • Every process page carries its complete ledger history, including who approved what and when.

I want to challenge a procurement decision

There are three routes, and none of them requires a lawyer to start:

  • Ask for a debrief. A losing bidder can ask why they lost, and the answer is published in the debrief log for that process.
  • File an objection. Any bidder or member of the public can object to an award inside the published objection window. Filing triggers a ten-day freeze of the procedure while an independent panel — half of it nominated by civil society — reviews the decision.
  • Report corruption. Anonymous reports are accepted at the whistleblower page, encrypted before storage, with a case reference so you can check what happened without identifying yourself.

The objection window is printed on the award itself. If it has closed, the freeze route is closed with it — that is a real limit, stated rather than hidden.

I want the data, not the website

Everything here is available as OCDS releases, JSON, CSV and a yearly zip — see the endpoints. There is no key, no quota and no approval step. If you are a journalist and would rather have a spreadsheet than an API client, export the register as CSV.

Accessibility and language

This site is built to be usable on the oldest phone and the slowest connection in the state, because that is where most of its users are. Concretely:

  • Every page works with JavaScript switched off, and no page depends on a mouse.
  • Text meets WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, focus is always visible, and no status is signalled by colour alone — the word is always there beside the colour.
  • Touch targets are at least 44 pixels, and pages are readable at 320 pixels wide without horizontal scrolling of anything except wide data tables.
  • Every table has a caption and proper headers, so a screen reader announces the right column for the right value.
  • Pages print: navigation and filters disappear, and links print their destinations.
  • Language: English and Hausa are supported in the registration and bidding flows. If you need another language, the Bureau's help desk can assist by telephone.

I have no smartphone, no data, or no computer

You are not excluded. The Bureau runs assisted bidding desks so that a contractor without a smartphone can still bid without paying an agent to do it for them: find the desk nearest you. Everything those desks do is recorded on the same ledger, under the same rules — the desk is a convenience, not a back channel.
